# Writing formats

Choose the relevant format and apply the request's conditions. These structures
help you start; they do not replace the requirements of a particular document.

## Reply, status, and report

Start with the result or specific problem. Then give the information needed to assess
it: what changed, why, how it was checked, and what material limitations remain.
Match the depth of explanation to the question. Separate completed work from plans
and assumptions. Do not recount the whole process when the reader needs its outcome.

When describing a change, including in a PR, show the final result so someone outside
the conversation can understand it. If useful, give an example of behavior before and
after the change. Do not present preparation as verification: a finished mockup does
not establish approval, and a successful application build does not establish a successful run.

## Article and teaching explanation

Identify the question the material answers and the reader's starting knowledge.
Orient the reader at the beginning: what this is about and why it is useful. Explain
new concepts as needed, connect claims with examples, and show material exceptions.

For a beginner, start with a familiar situation; for a specialist, with a distinction
or a new problem. Do not replace explanation with a list of terms. End with a conclusion
or way to apply the material if it adds meaning. Preserve stories and the author's
voice when they help the reading; do not turn every article into step-by-step instructions.

## Interface and errors

The text belongs to the current screen and an action available to the user. A button
names an action; a result message states the actual outcome; an error says what failed
and what next step is truly available. Do not hide a material condition in a tooltip.

Do not blame the person or invent a cause for a failure. Do not promise an automatic
retry, saved data, or email delivery without that contract. Preserve identical wording
for states that must remain indistinguishable to protect account information.

For an empty state, distinguish no records, no search results, and a loading failure.
These are different facts with different actions. Do not show “Nothing here yet”
in place of a loading error.

Use the interface's language and exact names. Keep the button, hint, and instructions
consistent. If strings belong to a localization system, follow its rules; do not
change technical keys, parameters, or available actions to make a phrase more elegant.

## Documentation and instructions

State the purpose, material prerequisites, and expected result. Put steps in execution
order; obtain commands, versions, paths, and permissions from verified sources.
Place a warning about an irreversible outcome before the corresponding action.

Each topic needs a main document. In an overview, keep the necessary introduction
and link to details. When moving or removing material, fix incoming links.
Do not create a new file without a distinct reader need.

If mandatory wording cannot change, preserve it and keep any explanation separate.
Do not claim to have tested a command when you only edited its text.

## Work email and unsolicited outreach

In a work email, establish the subject, request, or decision quickly. State the deadline
and response needed when known. Introduce an attachment by its purpose: what to look for in it.

For an unfamiliar recipient, briefly explain who is writing, why to this person,
the possible benefit, and a simple proposed next step. Mention only real connections;
do not invent an acquaintance, referral, or the recipient's interest. Allow for the
possibility that the offer is unsuitable. Do not turn the email into a long monologue
or request a time-consuming meeting without a clear reason.

Editing an email ends with a draft unless sending has been requested separately.
In English, a direct “Please send…” can be polite without stacked softeners such as
“We would like to kindly ask…”. Retain tact when the relationship or request calls for it.

## About a person, company, or product

First name an understandable category of work, the audience, and the benefit. Then
show support: what you can do, what you have done, your role, and where to see the result.
Select experience to answer the reader's questions, not to reproduce the author's full biography.

Test a stated value or advantage by asking “How does this change actions?” If
“responsibility” appears in no condition or action, the word explains nothing.
Do not infer a guarantee from a single certificate or many years of experience.

For a landing page, a useful sequence is: introduce the product → demonstrate value
in the chosen scenario → give material details and limits → state terms and an action.
Start planning with what can be shown, then write the explanation. Do not promise
a feature merely because the audience would find it useful.

A useful review describes the situation, how something was used, the result, and
limitations. Do not invent testimonials. If none exist, use a verifiable demonstration.

## News and press release

Find an event that matters to the audience. The heading and opening state what
happened and what it changes. Details, comments, and background follow.

Adjectives alone cannot turn internal company activity into a significant event.
A quotation belongs when it adds a position or information; do not invent a real
speaker's words. Facts and conditions of product availability must be verified.

## Slides

First distinguish a live presentation from a document intended to be read independently.
During a talk, the slide supports speech; when circulated, it must make sense without a speaker.

Keep one coherent block of meaning on a slide: a heading with the topic or conclusion,
supporting material, and necessary explanation. Do not place two unrelated ideas together
to fill space. If the topic does not fit, divide it by meaning and give each part an
accurate heading. An illustration's caption explains its significance rather than repeating
what is already visible.

## Worked example

Teaching data for a report: the catalog layout has been prepared; the client has not approved it.

Result: “The catalog layout is ready. The client has not approved it yet.”

Two sentences are enough for this message. It does not need the history of preparation,
a promotional adjective, a separate conclusion, or a claim that “the catalog is ready to print.”
