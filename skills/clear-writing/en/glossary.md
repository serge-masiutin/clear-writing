# Working glossary

Terms used to apply the skill. All definitions are included here;
familiarity with the books is not required.

- **Counterexample** — a likely mistake or misapplication that makes the limits
  of advice visible. [Explanation](chapters/ch03-explanation-evidence.md).
- **Prior knowledge** — the knowledge, experience, and expectations that author and
  reader bring to a topic; these need not match. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Reader contract** — orientation to the material's purpose, audience, and material
  conditions. It need not be a formal block. [Structure](chapters/ch04-structure.md).
- **Supporting tasks** — the specific explanations and material needed to achieve
  the text's purpose. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Illustration** — a way of showing an idea, from a written example to a diagram
  or demonstration. It need not be an image. [Presentation](chapters/ch05-presentation.md).
- **Context** — the circumstances in which a message is received: channel, timing,
  relationships, reputation, expectations, and the reader's state. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Module** — a self-contained unit of meaning suitable for selective reading;
  comparable items use similarly structured modules. [Structure](chapters/ch04-structure.md).
- **Unsupported evaluation** — a conclusion readers are asked to accept without
  a clear basis or their own experience. [Words](chapters/ch02-words-sentences.md).
- **Reader benefit** — the reason someone would choose to read the text;
  benefits can be practical, social, or emotional. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Unexplained fact** — accurate information whose significance is unclear to
  readers without explanation or comparison. [Explanation](chapters/ch03-explanation-evidence.md).
- **Warning words** — words and constructions that may not serve the meaning in
  a given context; prompts for review, not a universal ban. [Words](chapters/ch02-words-sentences.md).
- **Tone** — the author's attitude to the subject and reader, expressed through
  content, word choice, and presentation. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Text purpose** — the change in the reader's understanding or decision that the
  author seeks. Distinct from reader benefit and the business goal. [Reader](chapters/ch01-reader-context.md).
- **Cliché** — a familiar, ready-made expression that can hide specific meaning
  behind a conventional phrase. [Words](chapters/ch02-words-sentences.md).
